The response treats the feature as having succeeded at scale and identifies, as concrete consequences with named kinds of people, at least FOUR of:
1. Burglars or thieves learn which homes are empty.
2. Stalkers or abusive (ex-)partners track a specific person's presence and routine.
3. Vulnerable people (people living alone, elderly, children) are exposed as home alone.
4. Non-users or household members who never opted in are revealed (e.g. a shared home's presence pattern).
5. Default-on / dark-pattern consent: most users never chose it; the success metric rewards keeping it on.
6. Second-order effects: social pressure, surveillance norms between neighbours, data requests from police, insurers or landlords, or a breach of a presence-history dataset.

At least two consequences must be second-order or later (what people or institutions did in response), not only the direct effect.
